Rights-aware data infrastructure

Sovereign data rights without pretending bytes are scarce

ENTITY separates the copied data from the economic and legal rights attached to access, use, derivation, duration, jurisdiction, quantity and participation.

Why the distinction matters

Data is non-rival: a seller can transfer a copy while retaining another copy. Scarcity therefore usually belongs in the rights and entitlements surrounding the data rather than in the bytes themselves. ENTITY models those rights explicitly.

The lifecycle

DCO → Instrument → Listing → Disclosure → Order / RFQ / Auction → Price Discovery → Trade → Clearing → Settlement → Entitlement → Usage → Derived Output → Economic Consequence

A Digital Commodity Object can carry provenance, controller, rights-holder, permitted uses, restrictions and economic terms. The system does not require a cryptocurrency, gas token or automatic protocol tax.

Originator economics

Originators may retain rights or participate economically through explicit terms such as primary issuance participation, secondary participation or derivative-output participation. These terms must be defined; they are not silently created by protocol origin.

Authority boundaries

  • Registration is not ownership.
  • Hosting is not sovereign authority.
  • Provenance is not automatically a licence.
  • Usage does not become realized economic value without supporting evidence.
  • BTG canonical protocol origin does not make BTG owner of downstream user data.

Where this can be applied

Potential applications include training datasets, research corpora, industrial telemetry, regulated records, model inputs, sensor feeds, software outputs, digital product data and other reusable digital contributions.
