# Applications Across Regions and Countries

Public discussion draft — 20 October 2026

# One architecture, different regional needs

The following are **illustrative research opportunities**, not evidence of deployed BTG projects, public endorsements, data ownership, verified customer demand or regulatory clearance. Each example requires legal rights, measurable outputs and willing local partners.

## Rural and remote communities worldwide

Cooperatives, local businesses and researchers could qualify opt-in aggregate demand signals, logistics and service-access research, and locally developed algorithms. Evaluate connectivity constraints, exclusion of isolated individuals from aggregated results, language access and control over community data. The model is relevant to remote regions in any country, not only the Boundary region of British Columbia.

## Large urban regions

With appropriate agreements, privacy-protected aggregate signals could help businesses research accessibility gaps, local product demand or service design. Avoid exposing movement trails, small-cell re-identification or discriminatory profiling. Urban scale creates both more research possibilities and more privacy risk.

## Agricultural and food-producing regions

Rights-cleared research about crops, equipment, supply-chain reliability and market demand could help small growers and researchers. Farmers' commercially sensitive information and Indigenous/community land knowledge require separate permissions and safeguards.

## Industrial and manufacturing regions

Virtual research farms could test robotics recovery strategies and equipment algorithms. Independent reproducibility, safety engineering, vendor IP rights and customer evidence are essential before any asset is offered commercially.

## Education, science and research regions

Research institutions could contribute reproducible methods or qualified software components under approved agreements. Ethics review, minors' protections, export restrictions and institutional ownership rules take precedence over possible revenue.

## Public-service and disaster-resilience regions

Synthetic or properly authorized operational data could support validated tools for resilient infrastructure. Do not use emergency-response personal records, sensitive locations or operational command information by default. Independent public safety validation is mandatory for critical uses.

## Regions with constrained digital infrastructure

An adoption programme must be viable with low bandwidth, intermittent power, mobile-first access, offline workflows and transparent compute/energy trade-offs. No community should be assumed to have the same connectivity or technical resources as a major technology centre.

## Cross-border research partnerships

Independent institutions might collaborate on qualified, non-sensitive data products under explicit licences. Cross-border transfer, data localization, export controls, restrictions on onward use, and payment/tax obligations require separate analysis for each pair of jurisdictions.

## How to choose a first regional use case

Use five tests: clear buyer problem; rights-cleared lawful inputs; controllable privacy and cultural risks; independent reproducibility; plausible unit economics after payments, compute, assurance and support. An interesting dataset alone is not enough.
