Reality & Evidence

Layer boundary: this page explains later/current runtime evidence architecture. Protocol 1.0 validity is defined by the sealed Protocol 1.0 profile and conformance material.
REALITY → OBSERVATION → CLAIM → EVIDENCE → ATTESTATION → VERIFICATION → GOVERNED ENTITY STATE

Reality is external. An observation is captured by a process. A claim states an interpretation. Evidence supports, contradicts or contextualizes a claim. An attestation attributes a statement to an identified authority/witness. Verification evaluates the applicable integrity, protocol and evidence rules without collapsing those scopes.

Three verification boundaries

  1. Cryptographic verification: do the required hashes/signatures/commitments verify?
  2. Protocol verification: do records satisfy the applicable ENTITY target's schemas, authority and state-transition rules?
  3. Reality/evidence assessment: does the available evidence justify the claimed external-world conclusion for the stated scope?

A record can be cryptographically valid and protocol-valid while an external-world assertion remains false, disputed or insufficiently evidenced.

Contestability

Contradictory evidence and later conclusions should be representable without deleting earlier signed records. The evidence chain should show what was asserted or concluded, by whom, from which evidence, and how later state changed.
