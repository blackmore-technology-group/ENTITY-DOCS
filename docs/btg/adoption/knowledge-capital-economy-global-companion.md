# BTG Knowledge Capital Economy — Global Adoption Companion

**Blackmore Technology Group Limited | 20 October 2026 | Public discussion draft**

**Based on:** *Building a Knowledge Capital Economy: How an interconnected system for research, executable invention, lasting knowledge, lawful rights, commerce and investment is being engineered in British Columbia* (BTG, October 2026, 35-page institutional/community submission). This is a public-facing adaptation of its thesis, not a reproduction of its full submitted PDF.

## Grand Forks is the beginning, not the boundary

The original submission explains a potential economic path from a regional innovation project to research, qualified software capabilities, commercial data rights, and eventually lawful investment in asset-based enterprises. That model is not geographically tied to Grand Forks, British Columbia or Canada: its technical methods may be evaluated across regions, while **every participating jurisdiction must determine its own legitimate data rights, privacy duties, commercialization permissions and financial law**.

## The same seven transformations
1. **Distributed invention:** research with NIKI and independently qualified FarmNodes, including retained failures.
2. **Executable knowledge products:** DataChips, AI Chips, Circuits and versioned Circuit/Farm Genomes.
3. **Portable rights and authority:** ENTITY/ADAM permissions, provenance and actual underlying legal documents.
4. **Cumulative knowledge capital:** BTDU preservation, reconstruction and reusable verified learning.
5. **Asset-first enterprise:** useful intellectual assets can contribute to real business formation and legally structured investment, not automatically create securities.
6. **Contributor participation:** valid voluntary agreements, identifiable entitlements and payment only from real reconciled receipts.
7. **Reinvestment:** commercial demand and authorized capital support more research under measurable controls.

## Prospective contexts worldwide

| Regional context | Possible qualified use | Matters to resolve locally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rural and remote districts | Water, equipment, agricultural methods, remote technical jobs | Infrastructure, training, affordability, connectivity |
| Manufacturing regions | Tested robotics and predictive maintenance DataChips | Factory safety, source rights, real customer outcomes |
| Agricultural and food-producing regions | Rights-cleared crop, soil, water, weather and equipment research | Seasonal validity, farm confidentiality, land governance |
| Metropolitan areas | Opt-in, properly aggregated service and demand research | Re-identification, profiling, consent, equality |
| Universities and research centres | Reproducible experiments and specialist algorithms | Institutional IP, ethics approval, technology transfer |
| Public-service and disaster-resilience institutions | Synthetic or lawfully authorized planning research | Procurement, records law, public safety validation |
| Indigenous and collectively governed communities | Locally chosen knowledge projects | Legitimate community authority, consent, sovereignty, ability to refuse |
| Regions with limited digital infrastructure | Local or intermittent-sync research facilities | Energy, capacity, accessible languages, independent operation |

These are examples, **not** active BTG deployments, signed customer contracts, government endorsements or promises of local economic benefit.

## A replicable regional adoption method

**Discover → Local consultation → Rights/privacy assessment → Synthetic demonstration → Independent evaluation → Bounded authorized pilot → Measurable commercial validation → Consider expansion.**

Every province, state, country and community authority uses its own evidence-based readiness worksheet. One jurisdiction's technical, legal or financial clearance never transfers automatically to another.

**Evidence before claims:** documented user permissions, valid data-source contracts, independently reproducible performance where claimed, verified infrastructure and environmental costs, genuine buyer demand, actual customer payments, and clear corrections. Local procurement and personal conflicts must be disclosed and managed appropriately.

## A distinction the public must understand

The **commercial use of a qualified dataset or algorithm**, **an enforceable contributor payment arrangement**, and **investment securities** are different things. An ENTITY signature is not proof of ownership; a DataChip test is not a regulator's authorization; a DCO identifier is not a market price; a public GitHub page is not an order book. Securities issuance and trading require additional legal and regulatory clearance in every jurisdiction.

## Public and proprietary boundaries

The BTG public documentation programme explains the technology and adoption possibilities. It does **not** publish proprietary Exchange scripts, matching logic, participant records, payment internals, live instrument listings or private ledgers. Those are outside this adoption workstream.
