Global Adoption Framework

Public discussion draft · 20 October 2026 · Blackmore Technology Group Limited
Document status: Global adoption concept. No country outside a specifically documented approved pilot is represented as operating or authorized, and no investment is offered.

A global framework, locally governed

BTG — Bit Trade Global is intended to serve contributors, researchers, organizations and buyers in many countries. The proposed economic principles can be shared internationally, but law, language, technology, rights, cultural expectations and access cannot be assumed to be the same everywhere. The first British Columbia pilots are a possible proving ground, not the boundary of the model.

What remains the same across regions

A voluntary participant chooses an authorized activity source and permitted purposes; a suitably isolated FarmNode may process approved inputs into research; NIKI, BTDU and DataChips may help qualify outputs; ENTITY records provenance and permission evidence; a customer may lawfully license a qualified output. Capital formation and securities trading are separately governed activities, not an automatic result of joining a farm.

What must adapt locally

  1. Law and access: privacy rights, children's protections, data portability, export controls, intermediary/platform terms, intellectual property, financial rules, consumer protection and mandatory accessibility requirements.
  2. Community authority: Indigenous, tribal, traditional, local and collective data-governance interests where relevant, without assuming one country's governance model fits another.
  3. Language and culture: locally reviewed notices, accessible participation tools, dispute handling and communication appropriate to the region.
  4. Infrastructure: affordable devices, connectivity, compute location, energy cost, secure hosting, resilience and local support.
  5. Economic reality: market demand, local currency, banking/payment availability, tax, contributor compensation, business procurement and the administrative costs of a pilot.
  6. Accountability: local responsible organization, independent technical/privacy review, escalation pathways, audits, consultation and a prohibition on unsupported endorsements.

Replicable local adoption cycle

Discover → Co-design → Qualify → Pilot → Evaluate → Decide whether to expand. Every jurisdiction begins in the discovery stage. No pilot moves to the next stage just because a different country has passed it.

A regional team first identifies a legitimate local research use case and an interested buyer. The team then maps legal rights and infrastructure, consults people whose information or community interests could be affected, and performs a small pilot using synthetic or fully rights-cleared inputs. Only measured usefulness and independent safeguards justify wider participation.

Public evidence and success measures

Publish jurisdiction and version of the applicable policy assessment, supported source types, status of independent reviews, consent comprehension and revocation results, privacy incidents (appropriately aggregated), delivered research quality, verified buyer payments, contractual participant distributions and energy/compute costs. Show zero results as zero rather than projecting returns.

Relationship to the Exchange

The documentation and adoption programme does not create issuer records, tickers, ledgers, instruments, or live trading. Dedicated engineering teams own those components. This framework documents only the public pathway for lawful use, adoption, evidence, partnerships and confidence.

Current status

This is an international adoption design, not evidence of operational deployment in other countries. As of the 20 October 2026 public draft, jurisdiction-specific permissions, partners, and independent rollout evidence are not established by this publication.
