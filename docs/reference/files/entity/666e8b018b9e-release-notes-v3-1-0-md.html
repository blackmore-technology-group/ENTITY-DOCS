# ENTITY v3.1.0 — Global Infrastructure and Data Economic Sovereignty

ENTITY v3.1.0 is a feature release built on the security-hardened, internally qualified v3.0.1 base. It keeps ENTITY Core small and adds independently versioned profiles for the semantic, institutional, privacy and topology pressures that arise when sovereign digital infrastructure crosses organizations and jurisdictions.

## Data Economic Sovereignty Doctrine

v3.1.0 formally adopts the **Data Economic Sovereignty Doctrine**.

Data may represent economically valuable productive capital, but ENTITY does not require artificial scarcity of information itself. Information can remain non-rival and technically copyable while scarce economic interests are expressed as explicit machine-verifiable rights, entitlements, capacities, durations, jurisdictions, usage quantities, derivation rights, participation rights and transferability constraints.

Those bounded interests may be issued, reserved, licensed, transferred, traded, cleared, settled, exercised, revoked, superseded or extinguished under their governing terms. Originator participation may continue through primary issuance, secondary exchange, derivative creation or later economic use only where the participation interest was validly established.

> **ENTITY establishes market infrastructure through which data can become measurable economic capital.**

> **ENTITY transforms data from an opaque digital possession into a provenance-backed economic asset whose rights can be defined, issued, exchanged, exercised and economically accounted for.**
## Global infrastructure profiles

The release adds implemented and tested profiles for:

- jurisdiction/domain overlays with effective dates, precedence, supersession and fail-closed conflict detection;
- governed semantic namespaces, immutable ontology terms and explicit signed crosswalks;
- multi-stakeholder standards governance with stakeholder-class diversity and recusal;
- purpose-bound access, selective retention/destruction, confidential provenance and proof-verifier interfaces;
- core/regional/edge/satellite/offline topology, partition checkpoints and conflict handling;
- cryptographic-suite transition with dual-sign migration and downgrade resistance.

These mechanisms are profiles above `ENTITY / AUTHORITY / RIGHT / EVENT / VALUE`; they do not turn infrastructure possession, ontology publication or governance participation into sovereign authority.

## Economic and legal boundaries

ENTITY v3.1.0 is not a cryptocurrency and introduces no mandatory protocol token or protocol tax. The open protocol does not declare copied bytes to be scarce property. EEP instruments must identify the bounded right or entitlement being exchanged and cannot silently transfer ownership of underlying information.

Protocol records do not themselves determine legal title, statutory meaning, accounting or market fair value, regulatory classification, or objective bank/payment truth. Those remain dependent on applicable law, external authorities/evidence and deployment context.

## External qualification still required

BTG-controlled cross-language reproducibility is complete for this profile set, but v3.1.0 does not claim unrelated third-party implementation, independent external cryptographic review, or deployment-specific legal/regulatory approval. Those remain external milestones.

v3.0.1 remains immutable release history. v3.1.0 supersedes it as the current feature release without rewriting the v3.0.1 tag or evidence.
