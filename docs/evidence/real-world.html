Contribution economy

The economic model no longer starts with a bounty.

PROBLEM → BTG FIX → ENTITY PROVENANCE → INDEPENDENT VALIDATION → UPSTREAM ACCEPTANCE → MEASURABLE CONSEQUENCE → COMMERCIAL INSTRUMENT → SETTLEMENT

Bounties remain a price signal

A conventional bounty states a possible price before the engineering work begins. ENTITY can record that promise and later connect acceptance to settlement, but the bounty is only one acquisition and pricing mechanism.

Acceptance can exist before pricing

A contribution can be created, lineage-recorded, reviewed, tested and accepted by an unrelated upstream project even when no bounty exists. That acceptance provides externally verifiable evidence of the contribution and its technical adoption; it does not by itself establish a monetary price.

Economic consequence is a separate stage

Support, sponsored fixes, implementation work, enterprise integration, security remediation, certified builds, analytics, future contractual rights or other agreed commercial relationships can be recorded later without rewriting the original contribution history.

Verified contribution record

What ENTITY can preserve about an accepted contribution.

Technical and provenance evidence

Problem identity, contributor identity, exact change-set, timestamps, Git identities, review history, CI/security results, upstream acceptance, accepted commit and reconstructable evidence can be bound into a persistent lineage.

Rights and economic context

The ledger can preserve the upstream licence, contribution class, any pre-negotiated commercial terms, realized cash, settlement evidence and later downstream consequences while keeping those facts distinct from provenance itself.

Important distinction: an accepted contribution is evidence that an unrelated upstream project accepted the work. It is not, by itself, a valuation, invoice, royalty, ownership transfer or obligation to pay BTG.

Commercial pathways

Verified contribution history can support commerce without retroactively changing open-source rights.

Sponsored or pre-negotiated work

A project, sponsor or enterprise can define compensation before engineering starts. ENTITY can bind the agreed criteria to the later accepted contribution and settlement evidence.

Services around accepted expertise

BTG can separately contract for implementation, support, integration, remediation, certification or related engineering where a customer chooses to purchase those services.

Contribution portfolios and analytics

Repeated accepted work can form a verifiable technical history that can support reputation, qualification, analytics and future contracting without claiming that the upstream source code itself became proprietary BTG property.

Designed for scale

The same evidence path can support the next contribution and the hundredth.

Published acceptance receipts under ENTITY/evidence/bounty/ are discovered automatically. Despite the historical directory name, records may be bounty-backed or pro-bono. Actual ENTITY evidence supersedes bootstrap metadata, and live GitHub observations enrich the public view without changing the sealed historical record.

Pre-priced work

Funded or promised work can enter with an advertised or contracted value while settlement remains independently evidenced.

Unpriced accepted work

Pro-bono contributions can still accumulate externally verifiable provenance, review, CI, acceptance and downstream history while realized cash correctly remains zero.

Later economic consequence

Where evidence supports it, later contracts, sponsorship, services, downstream usage or other commercial events can be linked to the contribution lineage rather than being inferred from the merge alone.

Rights boundary: ENTITY provenance records contribution history; it does not convert an upstream project's source into BTG-owned property, override an open-source licence, or create payment rights that were never agreed.
