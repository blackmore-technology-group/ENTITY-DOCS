# Individual Participation Guide

Public discussion draft — 20 October 2026

# Individual participation and control

This guide describes **how BTG Bridge is intended to work**. It does not mean Bridge is currently offering a qualified public enrolment service.

## Your choices would come first

A participant should be able to select **which service**, **which information category**, **which purpose**, **how long**, and **whether commercial use is permitted**. There must be an equally understandable way to refuse. Disconnecting an account should stop future ingestion, subject to technically and legally necessary processing windows.

## Information that must not be ingested by default

Passwords, authentication codes, bank accounts, payment card records, medical records, government ID documents, private messages and recordings, data about other people where rights are absent, and sensitive characteristics inferred from ordinary activity. Seemingly mundane searches can reveal sensitive personal information, so rules must assess content and likely inferences rather than rely on source labels alone.

## Before a contribution is eligible for any commercial product

BTG should document the service-provider terms, applicable laws, consent for each affected person where required, rights granted by the contributor, privacy risk, processing purpose, transformation applied and contractual commercial permissions. A user exporting their own platform history **does not automatically receive a right to sell derived information** in conflict with an enforceable platform agreement or other people's rights.

## How a participant should understand compensation

Eligibility for royalties or other compensation requires a lawful commercial agreement and actual revenue or other agreed economic trigger. A data record has no guaranteed market value. The basis for allocation, fees, deductions, payment timing, tax considerations and dispute channels should be disclosed before consent.

## Withdrawal, access and complaints

A future Bridge service should provide accessible history, correction requests, consent withdrawal, deletion subject to legal retention duties, notices about completed downstream uses, and human review of unresolved disputes. Rights that have already been legitimately granted cannot always be reversed retroactively; this must be clearly disclosed.

## Pilot entry requirements

No participant recruitment until data protection impact review, current platform-terms review, meaningful consent tests, independent security assessment, incident response procedures and service readiness are documented and approved.
