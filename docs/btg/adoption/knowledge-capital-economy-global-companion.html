GLOBAL PUBLIC DISCUSSION · OCTOBER 2026

From Grand Forks to a global knowledge economy

An international adoption companion to Blackmore Technology Group Limited's 35-page October 2026 submission, Building a Knowledge Capital Economy.

Proposal, not certification. No international deployment, government endorsement, completed cross-institution transaction, investment offer or regulated exchange permission is asserted here.

The seven transformations

  1. Authorized research and independently verified FarmNode inventions
  2. Reusable DataChips, Circuits and Genomes
  3. ENTITY rights and ADAM authorization
  4. BTDU cumulative, reconstructable knowledge
  5. Asset-first enterprise under actual law
  6. Fair contractual contributor participation
  7. Real demand and legitimate reinvestment

Different regions, different needs

Rural districts may prioritize agriculture and infrastructure. Manufacturing corridors may investigate machine reliability. Cities may study opted-in aggregated service demand. Universities can evaluate reproducible experiments. Indigenous communities must retain their own legitimate governance authority over collective knowledge. Every use requires independent review and an actual lawful basis.

The local adoption cycle

Discover → Co-design → Verify rights → Test → Independently evaluate → Authorize a bounded pilot → Measure outcomes → Consider expansion.

Privacy, language, connectivity, energy, procurement, contributor rights, data transfers and any securities-market activity must be separately assessed in every jurisdiction. A successful Canadian test cannot grant global regulatory permission.

Proof before promotion

Measure genuine research outcomes, independent qualification, user consent, customer payments, public costs and disputes. A technical signature is not legal title; a test is not a commercial launch; a published document is not regulatory authorization.
