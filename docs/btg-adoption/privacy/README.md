# Privacy and consent

Public information edition 0.1 | 20 October 2026

# Privacy is a condition of participation, not a commodity to waive by default

BTG Bridge is intended to let individuals contribute permitted activity, but **the Bridge's ability to retrieve information is not the same as permission to commercialize it**. Search logs, social engagement and apparently public posts can still contain personal information.

## Proposed control baseline

- **Purpose-specific consent:** separate collection, private analysis and commercial release permissions.
- **Minimization:** obtain only categories reasonably necessary for an explained purpose.
- **Third-party protections:** do not sell someone else's private communications merely because a participant holds a copy.
- **Sensitive exclusion:** exclude credentials, banking, medical, government identifiers and similarly sensitive records by default.
- **Rights and requests:** clearly explain inspection, correction, deletion and withdrawal procedures, subject to lawful retention and completed contractual obligations.
- **Security:** encrypt protected information, separate roles, avoid public access to raw data and test incident response.
- **Disclosure-risk testing:** aggregates can still leak identity; test re-identification risk before publishing research results.
- **Provider restrictions:** honor limits in platform export or API contracts, including applicable restrictions on derived data.

## Consent example

A person might permit analysis of selected shopping comparisons for a consumer-demand study. That does **not** authorize BTG to disclose their account identity, reuse unrelated direct messages or transfer every search query to a buyer. Consent must be granular, recorded and withdrawn for future operations where applicable.

## No hidden recordings

The proposed Bridge does not rely on clandestine microphone access, phone-call interception, stealth monitoring or bypassing device protections. Any recording feature would require a clear lawful basis and the applicable permissions of affected people.

## Applicable legal review

Canadian privacy obligations vary by activity, jurisdiction, entity and subject matter. British Columbia PIPA and applicable federal privacy rules require professional, fact-specific analysis. Publication of this statement does not attest that every privacy control has already been deployed or independently assessed.
