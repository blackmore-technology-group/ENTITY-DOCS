Architecture Comparison

ENTITY and blockchain provenance solve overlapping—but different—problems

A blockchain can provide replicated ordering, tamper-evident history and consensus over ledger state. ENTITY focuses on a different boundary: who has authority to act, what rights exist, what evidence supports a claim, how provenance survives derivation and recovery, and what economic consequences are explicitly authorized.

Where blockchain provenance is strong

Distributed ledgers are useful when participants need a shared append-only history, replicated consensus and independently verifiable transaction ordering. Those properties can support provenance, but they do not automatically answer whether a signer had authority over an asset, whether an external assertion was true, or whether a downstream participant owns or may use the underlying data.

ENTITY's separation

QuestionENTITY treatment
Who may act?Authority and delegation are explicit protocol state.
Where did this come from?Provenance and evidence are recorded separately from authority.
Who owns or may use it?Rights and entitlements require explicit state and governing terms.
Is an external claim true?Cryptographic validity does not silently become objective truth.
What happens after derivation?Usage, derived output and economic consequence can remain linked.
Can the authority survive infrastructure change?Sovereign export and recovery are first-class concerns.

No required native token

ENTITY does not require mining, staking or a native protocol token to establish its authority and rights semantics. The current canonical protocol also sets automatic protocol royalty to zero; economic participation requires explicit terms rather than arising merely from protocol ancestry.

Can ENTITY use blockchain?

Yes. A ledger can be one external anchoring, settlement or evidence mechanism in a deployment. ENTITY's architecture does not require the ledger to become the root of sovereign authority. Infrastructure and consensus participation remain distinguishable from the authority represented by ENTITY state.

What this comparison does not claim

This is not a claim that ENTITY replaces every blockchain, distributed ledger or consensus system. It identifies which questions ENTITY makes authoritative and which questions a ledger can complement. Deployment requirements determine whether a replicated consensus layer is useful.

Inspect the boundary

Review the released protocol and try to find a path where infrastructure possession, provenance or protocol ancestry silently becomes ownership or sovereign authority. Reproducible counterexamples are explicitly requested.
