# Adoption roadmap

Public information edition 0.1 | 20 October 2026

# A staged public-interest adoption plan

BTG's adoption strategy must be driven by real evidence and legitimate participant outcomes, not by the existence of an attractive trading interface.

## Stage 1 — Evidence baseline

Inventory implemented modules, review engineering qualification, document ownership and privacy risks, and publish truthful status summaries. **Gate:** independent reviewers can reproduce named demonstrations and identify unresolved limitations.

## Stage 2 — Education and dialogue

Make public whitepapers, plain-language manuals, a transparent product-status register and stakeholder briefings accessible. Meet consumers, researchers, Indigenous communities, privacy specialists, businesses and public institutions. **Gate:** documented stakeholder concerns and revised safeguards.

## Stage 3 — Controlled Bridge pilots

Conduct voluntary, limited pilots using sources with demonstrable legal permission, purpose-specific consent and privacy impact review. **Gate:** independently evaluated consent lifecycle, deletion/revocation behavior, security and usable research outputs.

## Stage 4 — Commercial research customers

Offer qualified, authorized research products to a small set of customers. Test value, repeat purchase, contractual rights and cash reconciliation. **Gate:** verifiable payments and service delivery, with contributor entitlements honored.

## Stage 5 — Authorized capital formation

Establish issuer structures and engage securities counsel, dealers and regulators as required. Separate restricted primary instruments from any future securities marketplace. **Gate:** lawful issuance and reconciliation evidence, without unauthorized secondary trading.

## Stage 6 — Jurisdictional expansion

Evaluate additional provinces and countries one by one: legal rights, privacy, marketplace obligations, tax, currency, data residency, sovereign/community protocols and operational capacity. **Gate:** jurisdiction-specific authorization and independently monitored outcomes.

Adoption metrics should include consenting participants, active accounts, usable qualified assets, buyers, realized customer revenue, contributor distributions, complaint rates, confirmed privacy incidents, cost to serve, retention and independently reproduced findings. Not all metrics should be public at individual level.
