A neutral, evidence-led approach to engagement

International Partnership Programme

Public discussion draft · 20 October 2026 · Blackmore Technology Group Limited
Document status: Global adoption concept. No country outside a specifically documented approved pilot is represented as operating or authorized, and no investment is offered.

Partner engagement without presumed endorsement

BTG's proposed international adoption requires responsible local collaboration. An inquiry or meeting is not a partnership, public endorsement, procurement commitment or regulatory permission.

Individuals and civil-society organizations

Offer plain-language briefings about potential benefits and risks, ask how participants would control consent, and establish accessible complaint and withdrawal expectations. Pay attention to power imbalance and digital exclusion; a financial incentive must never obscure risks.

Universities and independent laboratories

Invite clearly scoped replication of a synthetic or rights-cleared task. Publish results, including failures, methods and costs. Independent reviewers must control their own findings and should not be portrayed as certifying all BTG capabilities.

Regional economic-development groups and industry

Explore one practical local research need, prospective buyer acceptance criteria, workforce participation and measurable economic impact. Avoid invented employment or income multipliers.

Government and public institutions

Offer a neutral policy briefing for discussion of data rights, privacy, competition, securities classification and innovation. Follow public procurement rules and protect public-office impartiality. No person or government is named as a partner without signed publication authorization.

Indigenous and community-governed institutions

Respect applicable collective decision-making protocols and sovereign community control over protected knowledge; an individual's checkbox does not transfer collective authority. Do not presume that Western individual-ownership frameworks are universally applicable.

Regulated financial and legal intermediaries

Pursue documented guidance on separate securities, banking, AML, custody, exchange and consumer-protection requirements. Treat consultation as information gathering until a competent authority issues formal permission.

Three-stage partnership documentation

  1. Non-binding exploration: scope, confidentiality, publicity restrictions and designated contacts.
  2. Agreed pilot: participants, lawful inputs, measurable outcomes, costs, liability, data handling and stop criteria.
  3. Validated partnership: signed contract and independent evidence supporting public claims; specific permissions retained and reviewed.

Publication rules

The portal will report confirmed jurisdictions and approved relationships only after explicit authorization. Publish the same disclosure rules for Canadian and overseas partnerships. All marketing and economic claims must remain traceable to dated evidence.
