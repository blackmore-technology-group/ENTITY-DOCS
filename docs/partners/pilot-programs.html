ENTITY v3.4.3 · bounded institutional experiments

Four partnership programs designed to produce evidence

Each program starts with a narrow institutional problem, a defined integration boundary, and a success/failure rubric agreed before testing. A partner is not asked to endorse ENTITY or adopt the full stack.

1. ENTITY Agent Authority Lab

Question: Can an autonomous or AI-assisted system operate only within explicit delegated authority while preserving a complete, independently inspectable action, evidence and provenance chain?

Test flow: agent identity → delegated authority → permitted data/action scope → decision or proposed action → deterministic governed transition → evidence/provenance → revocation/recovery.

Good partners: authorization standards groups, AI-agent platforms, security organizations, enterprise AI adopters.

Success evidence: allowed actions succeed; prohibited actions fail closed; authority can be revoked; resulting state and evidence can be independently reconstructed.

Failure evidence: authority scope can be escaped, action provenance is ambiguous, AI output silently becomes authority, or recovery changes the governed result.

2. Sovereign Data Rights Lab

Question: Can real data move through organizational or infrastructure boundaries while explicit machine-readable rights, provenance, permitted use and downstream obligations remain attributable?

Test flow: controller/asset identity → rights instrument → access/query/train/derive permission → governed use → derivative lineage → evidence → economic or compliance consequence → provider migration/recovery.

Good partners: data spaces, sovereign-data programs, enterprise data owners, public institutions, market-infrastructure organizations.

Success evidence: rights are enforceable at the test boundary, provenance remains intact, unauthorized use is detectable or rejected, and recovery does not destroy rights history.

Failure evidence: rights disappear on transfer, lineage becomes unverifiable, provider migration breaks authority, or the same authoritative occurrence creates duplicate obligations.

3. Portable Organizational Authority Lab

Question: Can an established external organizational credential or trust assertion become a trusted input to persistent ENTITY authority without ENTITY replacing the issuer?

Test flow: external verified credential → ENTITY identity binding → organizational role → delegated authority → governed action → provenance → revocation/expiry → portable recovery.

Good partners: credential issuers, organizational identity systems, digital-trust frameworks, wallet ecosystems.

Success evidence: the external issuer remains authoritative for the credential, ENTITY preserves downstream scope and provenance, and revocation/expiry propagates correctly.

Failure evidence: ENTITY silently becomes the issuer, external revocation is ignored, organizational roles become over-broad, or portability destroys issuer attribution.

4. Independent BTDU Qualification Program

Question: Do BTDU representation and governed-information advantages survive independently selected workloads, methodology and comparison architectures?

BTG provides: current code, internal benchmark definitions, evidence and hypotheses.

Independent partner controls: workload selection, comparison systems, measurement methodology, statistical treatment and conclusions.

Good partners: universities, research labs, independent systems researchers, applied AI/data infrastructure groups.

Success evidence: independently reproduced advantages under clearly specified conditions.

Failure or boundary evidence: workloads where the advantage disappears, costs shift elsewhere, or alternative architectures outperform BTDU. Negative evidence is a valid project outcome.

Pilot charter requirements

  • Named problem owner and technical sponsor on each side.
  • Exact data, identity, authority and infrastructure boundary.
  • Pre-declared success, failure and inconclusive criteria.
  • IP, confidentiality and publication rules agreed before testing.
  • No implied endorsement from participation.
  • No conversion of internal BTG evidence into “independent validation” unless the independent party controls the relevant methodology and conclusion.
Preferred outcome: not a press release. A useful partnership produces a reproducible report, integration artifact, standards mapping, security finding, pilot result, independently controlled benchmark or other evidence that an outside organization can stand behind.
