About ENTITY

Persistent digital authority that can survive the application that created it.

ENTITY is a Canadian-designed, provider-independent digital authority protocol created by Blackmore Technology Group Limited. It is designed to keep identity, delegated authority, provenance, rights, evidence, governed information, portable state, recovery and economic consequence understandable and verifiable even when applications, infrastructure providers, custodians or AI systems change.

The shortest definition: ENTITY is the authority layer that answers who or what is this, who is allowed to act, what information or rights are involved, where did they come from, what evidence supports them, what changed, and can the resulting state be independently recovered and verified?
The problem

Modern digital systems usually keep authority trapped inside providers.

Accounts live in one platform, permissions in another, data lineage in logs, licence terms in documents, AI decisions in model output, and recovery in proprietary backups. When those systems change, the meaning of the digital state can become difficult to reconstruct.

Identity is not enough

Knowing which account acted does not prove what authority it held, who delegated that authority, what limits applied, or whether the authority survived a platform migration.

Provenance is not ownership

Knowing where information came from does not automatically establish permission, legal ownership, commercial rights, truth, or entitlement. Those concepts must remain explicit and separately evidenced.

AI should not mint authority

An AI system can reason about evidence or recommend actions, but the model itself should not become the source of identity, permission, ownership, rights or settlement.

The ENTITY mental model

Authority first. Information, rights, evidence and value attach to that authority.

IDENTITY → AUTHORITY → INFORMATION → RIGHTS → PROVENANCE → EVIDENCE → GOVERNED EVENT → ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE → RECOVERY

Persistent identity and delegated authority

ENTITY can represent persistent actors, applications, organizations and other governed entities, together with explicit delegation, authorization boundaries and verifiable authority relationships.

Governed information and provenance

Information can be bound to exact lineage, source relationships, derivations, evidence and reconstructable state without treating provenance alone as proof of truth or ownership.

Rights and permitted uses

Rights can be represented separately from the bytes themselves: for example inspection, access, query, training, derivation, redistribution, commercialization or sublicensing where those rights legitimately exist.

Evidence, consequence and recovery

ENTITY can connect an event to its evidence, technical or economic consequence, receipts and recovery state so a later system can verify what happened rather than trusting a database row or application screen.

The technology stack

ENTITY is the authority system. The surrounding components have deliberately separate jobs.

ENTITY

Authority, identity, rights, provenance, evidence, economic state and recovery. ENTITY defines the governed relationships and the evidence required to verify them.

BTDU

Blackmore Technology Data Universe. BTDU 3.4.2 is the governed information layer used to preserve exact information objects, relationships, lineage and reconstructable state under ENTITY authority. Controlled BTDU benchmark campaigns have also measured substantial storage reduction while preserving exact recovery.

ADAM

Deterministic state transformation and replay. ADAM applies and recovers governed state transitions. It does not independently decide who owns something or who is authorized.

NIKI

Reasoning inside authority boundaries. NIKI can reason over governed evidence and information, but it is not allowed to create authority, ownership, rights or settlement merely because a model inferred them.

Separation matters: reasoning can change; providers can change; applications can change. The authority and evidence chain is intended to remain independently understandable.
BTDU measured engineering results

Storage reduction and exact destruction recovery have both been tested.

BTDU is not only intended to retain lineage. Controlled engineering campaigns have tested whether governed information can be represented more compactly and whether the accepted state can still be recovered exactly after the original working source is no longer available.

97% lower storage in the tested benchmark

In the controlled BTDU benchmark referenced by the v3.4.x engineering campaign, the tested representation required approximately 97% less storage than the comparison baseline while preserving the information needed by that workload. This is a measured result for that test configuration, not a universal compression guarantee for every dataset or workload.

100% exact recovery in the qualified destruction test

BTDU cold-recovery qualification demonstrated complete reconstruction of the qualified accepted source and evidence after removing dependence on the original working source. In the Memnox #46 qualification, the source clone, Git, GitHub and network were not used; the archive, manifest and upstream-acceptance proof reconstructed exactly, and all 865 of 865 tracked Git blobs verified with zero missing paths, zero extra paths and zero mismatches.

INGEST → GOVERNED BTDU STATE → ORIGINAL WORKSPACE NOT REQUIRED → COLD RECOVERY → EXACT HASH / BLOB VERIFICATION
Claim boundary: “97%” refers to the controlled storage benchmark that produced that measurement. “100% recovery” refers to exact recovery of the qualified test corpus and evidence, not a claim that every conceivable damaged or incomplete system state is automatically recoverable.
Data and economic infrastructure

ENTITY is designed to make digital rights economically legible—not to pretend copies of data are scarce.

Digital information can be copied. The economically meaningful object is often the governed right to do something with that information. ENTITY therefore separates the information itself from authority, usage rights, provenance, evidence, licences, settlement and downstream consequence.

DCO → INSTRUMENT → LISTING → DISCLOSURE → ORDER / RFQ / AUCTION → PRICE DISCOVERY → TRADE → CLEARING → SETTLEMENT → ENTITLEMENT → USAGE → DERIVED OUTPUT → ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE

Rights, not just copies

A governed object can describe what a party may inspect, access, query, train on, derive from, redistribute, commercialize or sublicense—subject to the actual rights and licences that exist.

Value can follow proof

A contribution or information asset does not need a bounty or pre-existing price in order to have provenance and acceptance evidence. Commercial terms can be created separately and later connected to the verified history.

Settlement remains evidence

ENTITY does not infer payment from technical acceptance. Revenue, royalties, sponsorship, entitlement or other economic outcomes require their own evidence and recorded settlement state.

Real-world evidence

ENTITY is now being exercised against events outside BTG's own software.

The public contribution ledger records external software work through exact lineage, review, CI, upstream acceptance and economic state. Vector and Memnox are current examples where unrelated upstream projects accepted BTG-authored contributions and the resulting evidence was recorded through ENTITY.

What that demonstrates

ENTITY can bind a real external problem, contributor, exact change-set, review evidence, CI result, accepted upstream state and later recovery evidence into one persistent contribution history.

What that does not demonstrate

Upstream acceptance does not create an invoice, royalty, ownership transfer or automatic monetary valuation. Commercial consequence remains a separate evidenced stage.

What ENTITY is not

ENTITY is infrastructure, not a claim that one technology replaces everything else.

Not a cryptocurrency

ENTITY can represent economic rights, value events and settlement evidence, but it is not defined by a token or speculative asset.

Not merely a blockchain

The core objective is verifiable authority, provenance, rights and recoverable state. A particular ledger technology is not the definition of the protocol.

Not an AI model

Models can operate within ENTITY-governed boundaries, but reasoning output is not automatically authority or truth.

Not just a database

A database stores state for an application. ENTITY is concerned with preserving the meaning, authority, evidence and recovery of governed state across applications and providers.

Not automatic ownership

Registration, ingestion, provenance or canonical lineage does not manufacture ownership of third-party information or override an upstream licence.

Not automatic value

ENTITY can preserve evidence needed to support valuation or settlement, but it does not declare a monetary value merely because something was recorded.

Provider independence and sovereignty

“Sovereign” means the governed state is not intended to depend on one provider account.

ENTITY's sovereignty language refers to portable, independently verifiable digital authority and recoverable state. It does not imply governmental sovereignty, government endorsement, legal immunity or jurisdictional supremacy.

Portable state

Authority and evidence should be exportable, recoverable and independently verifiable rather than disappearing when a vendor account, application or cloud service is unavailable.

Interoperability objective

Protocol conformance is intended to allow unrelated implementations to reproduce the same governed result from public protocol material. Independent external interoperability remains a separate evidence gate and is not assumed merely because BTG-controlled implementations agree.

Open source

The core ENTITY repository is published under Apache-2.0 so outside developers and organizations can inspect, test, reproduce, fork and independently implement public protocol material.

Canonical origin

Origin is provenance—not automatic ownership.

Shawn Blackmore → Blackmore Technology Group → ENTITY

The canonical lineage preserves the project's origin and stewardship history. Under the current canonical-status rules, replacing the root originator, replacing BTG as steward or removing the verified origin lineage means a derivative should not be described as canonical ENTITY.

Canonical origin does not automatically give BTG ownership of downstream user assets, datasets, derivatives or payments. It does not create an automatic protocol royalty or transfer upstream ownership merely because data is registered, ingested, mirrored, verified or processed through ENTITY.
